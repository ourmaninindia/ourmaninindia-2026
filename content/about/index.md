I was born in the sixties, so it’s no surprise that I gravitated toward books like Kerouac’s _On the Road_, Steinbeck’s _Travels with Charley_, Michener’s _The Drifters_, and most of Hemingway’s works. The members of the Beat Generation became known as bohemian hedonists, celebrating non-conformity and spontaneous creativity. I relate to that spirit—though rather than a Dharma Bum, I see myself more as a Happy Warrior.

<blockquote>Afoot and light-hearted I take to the open road,<br>
Healthy, free, the world before me,<br>
The long brown path before me leading wherever I choose. </blockquote>

As a teenager, hitchhiking across Europe and living on a kibbutz taught me that the road itself is a test of wisdom. My [LinkedIn profile](#ourmaninindia) details my professional journey, but looking back, what stands out most are the rich and varied experiences along the way.

While the Beats were generally anti-academic, I’ve always embraced education wholeheartedly. I trained as a zoologist (B.Sc.) in Sydney, became a Financial Controller in Amsterdam, and earned my credentials as an Information Security Auditor (CISA) in the digital space. Attending INSEAD and Harvard was both stimulating and exciting. I’ve also presented at an academic conference on customer service in Singapore—where I made some lasting friendships. So, when I set up an e-learning portal, it felt natural to use a platform co-developed by Harvard and MIT.

I’ve always had a deep appreciation for technology. Transparency and agility are core values for me, which is why I’m a passionate supporter of open source software. Lowering costs is a nice bonus, but it’s really the freedom and innovation that excite me. When I’m not reading or out on my racing bike, you’ll likely find me behind my Linux laptop, exploring the latest in open source tech.
